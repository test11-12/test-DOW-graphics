# The copy

Every word the ten films put on screen, in the order a viewer meets it. This file is
written by `npm run copy` from `src/projects.ts` and each film in `src/scenes/`, so
edit the text there, not here.

For each win: the headline and the line under it, what the viewer is told to watch
for, the captions with the time each one appears, the takeaway that closes the
film, the description read to people who cannot see it, what the film cannot show,
and the sources.

## 01 · Fury

Anduril · Mojave Desert, California · Announced July 2026 · Air

**Headline.** No pilot. Live missile.

**Under the name.** A pilotless Air Force jet fired a live air-to-air missile over the Mojave. A human gave the order.

**Watch for.** Watch who gives the order. It is not the jet.

| At | Act | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Sortie | A YFQ-44A over the Mojave with a live AMRAAM under its wing. Nobody is on board. |
| 0:03 | Sortie | Anduril says it went from a clean sheet to first flight in 556 days. |
| 0:07 | Consent | The jet can run the firing sequence itself, inside limits a pilot sets. It cannot say yes. |
| 0:10 | Consent | A human operator says yes. |
| 0:13 | Weapon away | Missile away. The target is digital. The missile is not. |

**The viewer acts at 0:10.** Button: “Authorize release”. It releases itself after 6 seconds.

**Takeaway.** First flight in October. A live missile by July. The Air Force wants about a thousand uncrewed fighters, and this is one of the first two designs.

**For people who cannot see it.** A view from above of an uncrewed YFQ-44A jet, held in the middle of the frame while desert passes beneath it, with one missile under its wing. A dashed link runs to a human operator. The jet requests release, the operator authorizes it, and the missile leaves the wing and flies to a digital target marked by brackets. A grid of 556 dots counts the days from the start of development to first flight.

**What it cannot show.** The Air Force did not release the test date, the range of the shot, the altitude, where the operator sat or the simulated result. The 556 days is Anduril’s count.

**Sources.** [The Aviationist, 15 Jul 2026](https://theaviationist.com/2026/07/15/yfq-44a-cca-fires-aim-120-amraam/) · [Air & Space Forces, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/anduril-yfq-44-fires-live-missile-landmark-cca-test/) · [The War Zone, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.twz.com/air/yfq-44a-fury-fighter-drone-has-fired-its-first-aim-120-amraam-missile)

## 02 · Leonidas

Epirus · Camp Atterbury, Indiana · August 2025 · Land

**Headline.** Forty-nine drones. One pulse.

**Under the name.** Epirus says one pulse of high-power microwaves dropped a swarm of 49 drones at once, and that all 61 flown that day came down.

**Watch for.** Count what is still flying after the pulse.

| At | Act | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Four set pieces | Camp Atterbury, Indiana. Drones are flown at Epirus’s own microwave weapon while nine allied countries watch. |
| 0:01 | Four set pieces | Three from the left, three from the right. Then one of a pair, picked by someone in the audience. |
| 0:04 | Four set pieces | One dropped inside a pre-set safe zone. Then three at three ranges, all at once. |
| 0:07 | The swarm | Then the finale: forty-nine drones of two types, in one swarm. |
| 0:11 | The swarm | Missile interceptors would take them one at a time. Leonidas gets one pulse. |
| 0:13 | One pulse | One pulse. All forty-nine fall at the same instant. |

**The viewer acts at 0:13.** Button: “Fire the pulse”. It releases itself after 5 seconds.

**Takeaway.** Sixty-one went up and sixty-one came down, by the company’s count. A missile takes one drone. A pulse takes a sky full.

**For people who cannot see it.** A view from above of a test range with a microwave emitter at the bottom. Small groups of drones approach and are brought down in four short scenarios. Then forty-nine drones gather in one swarm. A single pulse sweeps out from the emitter and all forty-nine fall at once. A roster of sixty-one dots fills as the drones come down.

**What it cannot show.** The 61-of-61 count is Epirus’s own, from an invitation-only demonstration. Axios, the only press present, reported the 49-drone finale. No independent tally has been published.

**Sources.** [Epirus, 10 Sep 2025](https://epirusinc.com/press-releases/epirus-leonidas-high-power-microwave-defeats-49-drone-swarm-100-of-drones-flown-at-live-fire-demonstration) · [Axios, 28 Aug 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/28/epirus-indiana-drone-swarm-demo-leonidas)

## 03 · Ward 250

Valar Atomics · California to Utah · February 2026 · Land

**Headline.** A reactor that ships like cargo.

**Under the name.** The Air Force loaded a privately built nuclear reactor into three C-17s and flew it nearly 700 miles. Four months later it went critical.

**Watch for.** Watch the clock, then the calendar.

| At | Act | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Load | March Air Reserve Base, California. A nuclear reactor the size of a minivan goes up the ramp of a C-17. |
| 0:03 | Load | It travels as eight modules in three aircraft, with no fuel on board. |
| 0:07 | Fly | Operation Windlord: nearly 700 miles to Hill Air Force Base, Utah, in about two hours. |
| 0:11 | Fly | The Energy Secretary rides along. A critic calls it a dog-and-pony show. |
| 0:14 | Switch on | From Hill it goes to a state lab outside Orangeville. The fuel is loaded later. |
| 0:17 | Switch on | On June 18 the Energy Department says it has gone critical: the second of three reactors the White House wanted by July 4. |

**Takeaway.** A reactor used to be something you built where it stood. This one flew in and went critical in four months.

**For people who cannot see it.** A cutaway of a C-17 cargo aircraft with a reactor module moving up its ramp, beside the outline of a minivan for scale. Then a map of the route from March Air Reserve Base in California to Hill Air Force Base in Utah, crossed by three aircraft while a clock counts two hours. Then a calendar of dots counts 123 days from the flight to the reactor reaching criticality in Utah, sixteen days before a July 4 deadline set for three reactors.

**What it cannot show.** The reactor flew without fuel; where it was fueled is not reported. Reports of its power rating differ, so none is shown. Criticality here is a zero-power chain reaction, announced by the Energy Department on June 18, 2026. The July 4 deadline was for three reactors; this was the second.

**Sources.** [PBS NewsHour, 21 Feb 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/u-s-military-airlifts-small-reactor-for-the-first-time-as-trump-pushes-to-quickly-deploy-nuclear-power) · [Nuclear Newswire, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-06-22/article-8136/valars-ward-250-reaches-criticality-in-utah/) · [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/military/us-air-force-airlifts-complete-nuclear-reactor)

## 04 · Talon-A2

Stratolaunch · Pacific Ocean to Vandenberg · December 2024 and March 2025 · Air

**Headline.** Hypersonic, and it comes back.

**Under the name.** A rocket plane dropped from the world’s widest aircraft, passed Mach 5 and landed itself on a runway. Then the same airframe did it again.

**Watch for.** Follow one airframe through two flights.

| At | Act | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Single use | Stratolaunch’s first Talon-A was built to fly once. Its powered flight ended in the ocean. |
| 0:03 | Single use | One flight, one airframe. |
| 0:06 | Flight one | December 2024. Roc, the widest aircraft ever flown, releases Talon-A2 over the Pacific. |
| 0:09 | Flight one | The rocket lights. It passes Mach 5, flies itself to Vandenberg and lands on the runway. |
| 0:14 | Flight two | March 2025. The same airframe goes again, faster. |
| 0:18 | Flight two | The Pentagon’s test office calls it the first reusable hypersonic flight testing since the X-15 stopped in 1968. |

**Takeaway.** A hypersonic test bed that lands and flies again turns a rare event into a schedule.

**For people who cannot see it.** A side-on flight profile over the Pacific. A first test vehicle is released, climbs, and comes down in the ocean. Then the Roc carrier aircraft releases Talon-A2, which climbs under rocket power, passes a mark labeled Mach 5, glides down and lands on a runway at Vandenberg. The same vehicle is carried back and flies the profile a second time, faster; the second path is drawn slightly higher.

**What it cannot show.** Stratolaunch did not release exact speeds, and the reports give no altitudes or distances. Both flights passed Mach 5 and the second was faster. The profile is schematic.

**Sources.** [Stratolaunch, 5 May 2025](https://www.stratolaunch.com/news/stratolaunch-successfully-completes-reusable-hypersonic-flight-and-recovery-with-talon-a2-vehicle/) · [The Aviationist, 6 May 2025](https://theaviationist.com/2025/05/06/stratolaunch-tests-talon-a2-reusable-hypersonic-flight/)

## 05 · Varda W-5

Varda Space Industries · Koonibba, South Australia · January 2026 · Space

**Headline.** Every landing is a hypersonic flight test.

**Under the name.** A capsule spent 63 days in orbit, hit the atmosphere at about 30,000 km/h and landed under a parachute in the Australian outback.

**Watch for.** The streak across the sky is the experiment.

| At | Act | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Orbit | Launched from California on November 28, 2025, Varda’s W-5 capsule spends 63 days in orbit on the company’s own spacecraft. |
| 0:04 | Orbit | On board is a US Navy payload built to collect data during re-entry. |
| 0:07 | Re-entry | Early on January 30, 2026, Australian time, the capsule separates over Antarctica and hits the atmosphere at about 30,000 km/h. |
| 0:10 | Re-entry | People in Koonibba and Ceduna watch it draw a line across the night. |
| 0:14 | Recovery | A parachute opens. It comes down on the Koonibba Test Range, more than 41,000 square kilometers of outback. |
| 0:18 | Recovery | It is the third capsule to land here in under twelve months. |

**Takeaway.** A capsule that comes home on purpose is a hypersonic test range you can book.

**For people who cannot see it.** A night sky over South Australia, with stars drawn as dots above a flat horizon. A plate in the middle of the sky shows a spacecraft circling the Earth while a counter reaches 63 days; it then shrinks into the corner and shows a capsule leaving the spacecraft over Antarctica. In the main picture the capsule crosses the sky as a bright streak, marked at equal steps of time that bunch up as it slows, then descends under a parachute and lands inside a marked test range.

**What it cannot show.** Speed figures are Varda’s and Southern Launch’s. Varda says only that the Navy payload collected data during re-entry. Dates are Australian: in California it was still January 29. The sky and the track are schematic.

## 10 · Blackbeard

Castelion · US Navy flight test · September 2026 · Air

**Headline.** Hypersonic, by the thousand.

**Under the name.** A startup’s hypersonic missile flew under the wings of a Navy Super Hornet. The first production lot is 444 rounds. The Pentagon wants 12,000.

**Watch for.** Every dot is one missile. The lit ones are on order.

| At | Act | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | On the wing | September 25, 2026. The Navy posts footage of a Super Hornet in flight with Castelion’s Blackbeards under its wings. |
| 0:03 | On the wing | It has not given a speed, or said whether one has yet left the rail. |
| 0:06 | First lot | Eleven days earlier the Pentagon announced the first production lot: 444 missiles, with a first award of $43.65 million. |

| 0:13 | The ask | In May the Pentagon said it was seeking the money to buy more than 12,000 Blackbeards over five years. |
| 0:15 | The ask | What it committed to: at least 500 a year for two years, once the missile passes testing. |
| 0:18 | The ask | The Navy wants the first ones in service by 2028. |

**Takeaway.** Hypersonic used to mean exquisite and scarce. Twelve thousand, even as an ask, is an ammunition number.

**For people who cannot see it.** A Super Hornet seen from above with a missile under each wing. The missiles become the first of 444 dots that fill a block in the corner of the frame: the first production lot. The rest of the frame then fills with dimmer dots until there are 12,000, divided into five yearly bands: the number the Pentagon said in May that it is seeking to buy.

**What it cannot show.** The Navy has not given a speed or said whether one has been launched from the jet. No price per round has been published. The 12,000 is a goal the Pentagon is seeking money for; its commitment is at least 500 a year once testing is done.
