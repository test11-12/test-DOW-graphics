# Sources: what each film rests on

One entry per film. For each: what is reported and by whom, what is the maker's own
claim, what was not released, what the film works out for itself, where sources
disagree, and what is drawn rather than reported. The links a viewer sees on the page
are in `src/projects.ts`; this file is the longer account behind them.

The sources were read on 10 and 11 November 2026. Where a figure is given below without
a caveat, at least one of the linked sources states it. Some pages refuse automated
readers and were read only as other outlets quote them; each is named where it
matters. Two of the links in the original brief could not be read: Red Hot Cyber
(Leonidas) returns "not found", and has been replaced; Marine Log (Marauder) refuses
automated readers, so nothing here was checked against it. Axios (Leonidas) also
refuses automated readers: its report was read through a repost and a summary, and
should be opened before it is quoted.

Every film has been put through an independent check: a reader with no part in making
it took each caption, label and figure back to the sources and reported whatever was
not fully supported. Across the ten films that raised some fifty points, all applied.
Most were wording and attribution. Four were wrong, and are described where they occur
so that nobody repeats them: Blackbeard's 12,000 and its price per round, the standing
of LUCAS's price, and what Varda says of its Navy payload. A fifth, the order of
events in Victus Haze's second sortie, had been caught earlier.

## 01 · Fury

Sources: [The Aviationist, 15 Jul 2026](https://theaviationist.com/2026/07/15/yfq-44a-cca-fires-aim-120-amraam/); [Air & Space Forces Magazine, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/anduril-yfq-44-fires-live-missile-landmark-cca-test/); [The War Zone, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.twz.com/air/yfq-44a-fury-fighter-drone-has-fired-its-first-aim-120-amraam-missile). The Air Force's own release refuses automated readers; its words are given as these three quote them.

- **Reported.** The Air Force announced on 15 July 2026 that a YFQ-44A fired an AIM-120
  AMRAAM at "a digital target in secluded airspace over the Mojave Desert". Anduril said
  the aircraft flew from Edwards Air Force Base and that "an operator tasked the
  aircraft to engage the target, and YFQ-44A fired an AIM-120 as instructed". The Air
  Force: "CCA will not autonomously employ weapons", and the decision to release
  "remains exclusively with a human operator". Gen. Dale White: the aircraft "can
  execute the weapon employment sequence autonomously within pilot-defined
  parameters". First flight was on 31 October 2025. Inert captive-carry flights
  followed in February 2026, and the Air Force announced production orders for both
  Increment 1 designs in June 2026 (The War Zone: "last month"). The Air Force
  "eventually wants a fleet of about 1,000 CCAs": that is every such aircraft across
  several increments, not this type alone, so the film says "about a thousand uncrewed
  fighters, and this is one of the first two designs".
- **The maker's own count.** The 556 days is Anduril's: "from clean-sheet design to
  one-click takeoff in 556 days". The film attributes it. No source read names the day
  the count starts.
- **Not released.** The date of the test (the film gives the month it was announced),
  the range of the shot, altitude and speed, where the operator was, whether another
  aircraft was involved, and the simulated result. The film says so on the plate. No
  report says where the missile hung for this shot; the aircraft "carries stores
  externally on either of two hardpoints, one under each wing" (The War Zone), and the
  film draws it there. "Live" rests on the Air Force's "live fire test" and on Air &
  Space Forces; no source says what warhead, if any, the round carried.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The geometry of the shot, the wedge standing for
  "pilot-defined parameters", and the link to the operator are diagrams. The missile is
  drawn about three fifths the length of the jet, which is roughly their real
  proportion.

## 02 · Leonidas

Sources: [Epirus, 10 Sep 2025](https://epirusinc.com/press-releases/epirus-leonidas-high-power-microwave-defeats-49-drone-swarm-100-of-drones-flown-at-live-fire-demonstration); [Axios, 28 Aug 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/28/epirus-indiana-drone-swarm-demo-leonidas).

- **The maker's own count.** The demonstration was on 26 August 2025 at Camp Atterbury,
  Indiana, by invitation, before people from the Defense Department, other US agencies
  and nine allied countries. By Epirus's account sixty-one drones flew in five scenarios
  and all sixty-one were disabled: two groups of three from opposite sides; one of two
  close drones chosen by a member of the audience, then the other; one dropped inside a
  "software-defined, pre-identified safe zone"; three at different ranges
  "simultaneously"; and a swarm of forty-nine, of two drone types, defeated with one
  pulse. 6 + 2 + 1 + 3 + 49 is 61. Epirus says "one pulse" only of the swarm.
- **Independently reported.** Axios was the only press present. It reported that
  Leonidas faced 49 quadcopters and brought all of them down at once. It gives no
  61-drone total and does not count pulses.
- **Not released.** Range, altitude, drone models and timings. No outside tally has been
  published, and the film calls the count the company's.
- **Drawn, not reported.** Where each drone flew and exactly when; who flew the drones;
  and the order of the first four scenarios, which follows the release's list (the
  release does not number them, and says only that the swarm came last). The film
  draws seven engagements, one for each defeat the release describes. The caption's
  remark that missile interceptors take drones one at a time is a general statement,
  not a figure from the demonstration.

## 03 · Ward 250

Sources: [PBS NewsHour / AP, 21 Feb 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/u-s-military-airlifts-small-reactor-for-the-first-time-as-trump-pushes-to-quickly-deploy-nuclear-power); [Nuclear Newswire, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-06-22/article-8136/valars-ward-250-reaches-criticality-in-utah/); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/military/us-air-force-airlifts-complete-nuclear-reactor).

- **Reported.** On 15 February 2026 a Valar Atomics reactor, described as minivan-sized
  and carried without fuel, was flown from March Air Reserve Base, California, to Hill
  Air Force Base, Utah: nearly 700 miles in about two hours (PBS/AP). The operation was
  called Windlord; the reactor traveled as eight modules in three C-17s (New Atlas).
  Energy Secretary Chris Wright was aboard. Edwin Lyman of the Union of Concerned
  Scientists called the flight "a dog-and-pony show". The reactor went on to the Utah
  San Rafael Energy Lab, a state lab outside Orangeville. The Energy Department
  announced on 18 June 2026 that it had completed a zero-power criticality
  demonstration.
- **The deadline.** The 4 July deadline comes from the president's May 2025 executive
  order and was for several reactors, not this one: Nuclear Newswire calls this "the
  second reactor criticality tied to" the order and says the "three-reactor target by
  July 4 is still unmet" as of 22 June. The film says "the second of three reactors
  the White House wanted by July 4" and labels the margin "16 days before the
  deadline", not "to spare".
- **Arithmetic.** 15 February to 18 June 2026 is 123 days; to 4 July, 139; the margin
  is 16.
- **Where sources differ.** The reactor's power: PBS quotes Valar's chief executive at
  up to 5 megawatts; Nuclear Newswire describes the Ward 250 as a 100-kilowatt-thermal
  test reactor. The film gives no power figure and calls it a reactor, not a power
  plant. Nuclear Newswire dates the Energy Department's announcement, not the moment
  of criticality, so the film says the Department "says it has gone critical" on
  18 June.
- **Not reported.** Where the reactor was fueled. It flew without fuel and later went
  critical, so fuel was loaded in between; the film says only "the fuel is loaded
  later". No source names the county March Air Reserve Base is in; the map label
  (Riverside County) is ordinary geography.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The cutaway, the module's exact size, the ramp closing, and
  the route, which is a straight line between the two bases.

## 04 · Talon-A2

Sources: [Stratolaunch, 5 May 2025](https://www.stratolaunch.com/news/stratolaunch-successfully-completes-reusable-hypersonic-flight-and-recovery-with-talon-a2-vehicle/); [The Aviationist, 6 May 2025](https://theaviationist.com/2025/05/06/stratolaunch-tests-talon-a2-reusable-hypersonic-flight/).

- **Reported.** Talon-A2, an autonomous rocket-powered vehicle, flew hypersonic in
  December 2024 and again in March 2025, was released from the Roc carrier aircraft
  over the Pacific, passed Mach 5 on both flights and landed at Vandenberg Space Force
  Base. The second flight was faster than the first. Between flights some avionics
  were replaced and the thermal protection was refurbished. The first Talon-A (TA-1)
  was "a single use aircraft which splashed in the ocean at the end of its tests";
  one powered flight is on record, in March 2024, and the film says "its powered
  flight ended in the ocean". The Defense Department's Test Resource Management Center
  called the campaign "the Nation's first return to reusable hypersonic flight testing
  since the manned X-15 program ended in 1968".
- **From elsewhere.** Roc's wingspan, 385 ft, the largest of any aircraft flown, is
  Guinness World Records' figure; the two sources above say only "the world's largest
  wingspan".
- **Arithmetic.** 1968 to December 2024 is 56 years.
- **Not released.** Exact speeds: the company "did not release the exact numbers". The
  reports give no altitudes or distances either. The film's profile has no scale, and
  its speed bars end open beyond Mach 5.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The shape of the profile, Roc seen head-on, the path the
  vehicle is carried back along, and the second flight's path being slightly higher
  than the first: only "faster" is reported.

## 05 · Varda W-5

Sources: [Space Connect](https://www.spaceconnectonline.com.au/discovery/6934-community-and-astrophotographer-capture-spectacular-return-of-varda-space-capsule-over-sa); [Southern Launch](https://www.southernlaunch.space/news-feed/southern-launch-supports-third-orbital-re-entry-in-12-months-to-koonibba-test-range); [Varda, W-5 mission page](https://www.varda.com/mission/w-5).

- **Reported.** W-5 launched on 28 November 2025 on SpaceX's Transporter-15 from
  California, spent 63 days in orbit attached to Varda's own next-generation
  spacecraft, separated over Antarctica at 12:09 am on 30 January 2026, Australian
  time, re-entered at about 30,000 km/h and came down by parachute on the Koonibba
  Test Range in South Australia, a range of "more than 41,000 square kilometres" run
  by Southern Launch. The re-entry was photographed by astrophotographer Will Godward
  and by people in Koonibba and Ceduna. Southern Launch calls it the third capsule to
  land at Koonibba in under twelve months.
- **Two calendars.** Varda dates the re-entry "6:07 AM Pacific time on January 29,
  2026": the same moment, a day earlier in California. The film gives the launch on
  its California date and the re-entry on its Australian one, and its caption says
  "Australian time". The 63 days is the Australian sources' figure.
- **The maker's own account.** The capsule "carried a specialized payload for the U.S.
  Navy focusing on data collection during reentry"; this was the company's "fifth
  mission overall and fourth reentry"; the heat shield was made at Varda's
  headquarters from C-PICA. Varda's capsules enter at 18,000 miles an hour and reach
  "Mach 25+" on every mission: a figure for the series, not a measurement of this
  flight, and the rail's note says so.
- **Left out.** Varda's page quotes its own vice president of mission management:
  "With W-5, AFRL and Varda again demonstrated that hypersonic flight testing can be
  done routinely and affordably". That ties the Air Force Research Laboratory to this
  flight, but the page does not say what the laboratory flew, so the film does not
  name it.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The sky, the track across it, the spacing of the time marks
  and the landing point. The marks are laid down at equal intervals of film time to
  show slowing; they are not telemetry. "Outback" is the film's word for the range.
  That every landing is a hypersonic flight test is the film's own framing of a
  capsule that re-enters at Mach 25.

## 06 · LUCAS

Sources: [Ground News roundup](https://ground.news/article/in-a-first-a-us-navy-warship-in-the-middle-east-just-launched-a-one-way-attack-drone); [The War Zone, 18 Dec 2025](https://www.twz.com/air/american-shahed-136-clone-fired-from-navy-littoral-combat-ship); the US Navy's release as carried by [Naval News, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2025/12/u-s-navy-in-middle-east-employs-attack-drone-at-sea-for-first-time/); Central Command's release of [3 Dec 2025](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/552756/us-launches-one-way-attack-drone-force-middle-east); [Defense News, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/02/28/us-confirms-first-combat-use-of-lucas-one-way-attack-drone-in-iran-strikes/).

- **Reported.** The Navy: personnel of US Naval Forces Central Command and 5th Fleet
  "successfully launched a one-way attack drone from a ship at sea for the first time,
  Dec. 16", from the flight deck of USS Santa Barbara (LCS 32), an Independence-class
  littoral combat ship. The War Zone reports a rocket-assisted launch from the stern
  flight deck by Task Force 59. The claim of a first is the Navy's, not Central
  Command's, and it is "from a ship at sea"; the film now says so. LUCAS is built by
  SpektreWorks and derived from Iran's Shahed-136; it is about 10 ft long with a span
  of about 8 ft (The War Zone). Central Command announced Task Force Scorpion Strike on
  3 December 2025 and said the drones "can be launched with different mechanisms to
  include catapults, rocket-assisted takeoff, and mobile ground and vehicle systems".
  On 28 February 2026 Central Command confirmed the drone's first use in combat, in
  strikes on Iran (Defense News).
- **The price.** "Costing approximately $35,000 per platform" is in Central Command's
  own caption to its 3 December release, so the film attributes it to Central Command.
  An earlier build called it unofficial; that was wrong.
- **Not released.** How far the drone flew, how it was controlled, and whether it hit
  anything.
- **Arithmetic.** 16 December 2025 to 28 February 2026 is 74 days: "just over ten
  weeks".
- **Drawn, not reported.** The ship's profile, the launch rail, the flight path and the
  booster's fall. That a booster drops away is how rocket-assisted takeoff works; no
  source read describes it for this launch, and the rail's note says the fall is
  schematic. "Built for helicopters" on the flight deck is general knowledge about the
  class, whose deck is sized for two Seahawks; it is not from the reports.

## 07 · Quarterhorse

Sources: [Mobility Engineering Technology, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/55323-hermeus-quarterhorse-completes-first-uncrewed-supersonic-flight); [Hermeus, 26 May 2026](https://www.hermeus.com/article/hermeus-achieves-its-first-unmanned-supersonic-flight).

- **Reported.** Hermeus announced on 26 May 2026 that Quarterhorse Mk 2.1, an uncrewed,
  remotely piloted aircraft the size of an F-16 with a Pratt & Whitney F100 engine,
  reached Mach 1.21 on its third test flight, flown from Spaceport America over White
  Sands Missile Range. That was less than three months after its first flight and 364
  days after the first flight of the company's first aircraft, Mk 1. Mk 2.2 is being
  built.
- **The maker's own claim.** "The world's first privately-developed uncrewed
  supersonic jet". The film says "privately developed", and "Hermeus calls it".
- **From elsewhere.** The delta wing: the brief's word, and Army Recognition's
  ("a tailless delta-wing layout"); neither linked source describes the planform. No
  source read says the airframe has no cockpit, so the film says "nobody on board".
- **Not released.** The date of the flight itself, its altitude and its length.
- **Worked out, and exact.** The rings and the cone. In a picture that moves with the
  aircraft, a beat of sound is a circle growing at the speed of sound and drifting back
  at the aircraft's speed; above Mach 1 the circles lie inside a cone whose half-angle
  is the arcsine of 1 / Mach. At Mach 1.21 that is 55.7 degrees.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The acceleration from Mach 0.72 up to 1.21: only the top
  speed is reported.

## 08 · Marauder

Sources: [Naval News, 29 May 2026](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2026/05/saronic-launches-first-marauder-medium-unmanned-surface-vessel/), which carries Saronic's release; [WorkBoat, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.workboat.com/saronic-launches-second-marauder-at-louisiana-shipyard); [Marine Log](https://www.marinelog.com/?p=109514) (the link in the brief; it refuses automated readers, so nothing here was checked against it).

- **The maker's own account.** Saronic laid the keel of the first Marauder at its yard
  in Franklin, Louisiana, on 22 August 2025 (the date is Janes's) and announced its
  launch on 29 May 2026, saying the hull "moved from initial design to on-water trials
  in under a year". The release gives a "150-metric-ton payload capacity" and "a range
  of up to 5,400 nautical miles": two maxima, not a single loading, so the film says
  "up to" for each. The second hull was turned over in March 2026 and was afloat by
  early November 2026; Saronic's chief executive said it "required 8% fewer labor
  hours than the first". The third and fourth hulls were under construction in May;
  what they were in November is not reported, and the film's key says "in May". At
  full production the yard "is expected to build 20 Marauders annually". Saronic says
  the yard was closing when it took it over.
- **The length.** At keel-laying the ship was 150 ft (46 m) with 40 metric tons of
  payload and a range of 3,500 nautical miles. Saronic's own page now lists Marauder at
  180 ft; the launch reports give no length, and one November report still says 150.
  The film's 180 ft is labeled "as Saronic lists it".
- **Arithmetic.** 22 August 2025 to 29 May 2026 is 280 days, a little over nine
  months. No source gives the day the hull entered the water: the second date is the
  announcement, so the film says "about nine months, keel laid to launch announced".
  Twenty a year less the four hulls drawn is sixteen "to fill a year at full rate".
- **Not reported.** When the second hull was started, and the day it was launched. Its
  line on the calendar begins where it was turned over.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The hull form, the order of building, the waterjets, and
  the arrangement of containers on deck.

## 09 · Victus Haze

Sources: [True Anomaly, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.trueanomaly.space/newsroom/victus-haze-mission-update-jackal-hunts-puma) and [1 Jul 2026](https://www.trueanomaly.space/newsroom/first-victus-haze-mission-complete); [Rocket Lab, 22 Jun 2026](https://investors.rocketlabcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rocket-lab-shatters-responsive-space-record-launches-us-space) (refuses automated readers; read as other outlets carry it); [DefenseScoop, 23 Jun 2026](https://defensescoop.com/2026/06/23/space-force-victus-haze-launch-rocket-lab/).

- **Reported: the launch.** Rocket Lab launched its spacecraft on an Electron from
  Mahia, New Zealand, on 19 June 2026, 16 hours 42 minutes after the Space Force's
  notice to launch and "to a previously unknown orbit". The notice is the Space
  Force's; the film's label says so. The previous record, on Victus Nox in 2023, was
  27 hours (DefenseScoop). Rocket Lab calls the spacecraft Pioneer; Puma is True
  Anomaly's name for it. True Anomaly's Jackal had been in orbit since May.
- **Reported: the first sortie.** "Our sensors acquired the spacecraft within hours of
  its launch": custody came first. Then "Space Systems Command tasked us to execute
  this sortie within 72 hours. We completed it in 61": Jackal closed on Puma, made
  several circuits of it, photographed it from several aspects and returned to its own
  orbit. The 72 hours are for the sortie, not for finding the target, and the caption
  now says so.
- **Reported: the second sortie, in this order.** Space Systems Command tasked True
  Anomaly on 14 July. "Jackal's crew produced a validated mission plan in under 30
  minutes from tasking." "Jackal executed ingress and a burn sequence over 23 minutes
  to close on Puma." During the approach Puma maneuvered away; Jackal sent down what
  it saw; the ground team replanned, revalidated and implemented a new plan "in rapid
  succession"; Jackal made fresh burns to re-engage and image Puma. Puma maneuvered
  again, and Jackal stayed on it and completed the mission. So the 30 minutes and the
  23 minutes belong to the first plan and the first approach, not to the response to
  the escape, for which no duration is given. An earlier build of the film had this
  the wrong way round.
- **Not said.** That custody was lost. True Anomaly says Puma fired its thrusters "to
  slip out of view" and that Jackal "keeps chase"; the film's log says "out of view".
- **The maker's own claim.** "The first company ever to execute a space-to-space
  pursuit of an evading target" (True Anomaly).
- **Not released.** Every altitude and distance, how close Jackal came, and how long
  the replanning took.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The whole geometry. The model keeps one true rule (the lower
  orbit is the faster one) and the reported durations and order; the size of the
  orbits, the direction of Puma's burn and the shape of the chase are illustrative.
  The film shows one escape in the story; the second is left to the viewer's button,
  and any further burns are the model's, not the mission's.

## 10 · Blackbeard

Sources: [The War Zone, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.twz.com/air/blackbeard-hypersonic-missile-seen-flying-on-f-a-18-super-hornet-for-the-first-time); the [Department of War's release of 13 May 2026](https://www.war.gov/news/Releases/Release/Article/4485332/department-of-war-enhances-lethal-strike-capacity-through-partnership-with-new/) (refuses automated readers; read through a mirror and against [Castelion's release](https://www.castelion.com/news/castelion-and-department-of-war-sign-agreement/) of the same day); [Naval News, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2026/09/castelion-secures-u-s-navy-production-order-for-blackbeard-hypersonic-weapon/).

- **Reported.** On 25 September 2026 the Navy released footage of flight testing with
  "Blackbeard hypersonic strike missiles under the wings of F/A-18E/F Super Hornets".
  On 14 September the Pentagon's daily contract notice gave a Navy order "valued at
  $43.65 million" for "the first production lot of 444 All-Up Rounds"; the Navy's
  release the next day described a production delivery order "with a total value of up
  to $200 million". The Navy confirmed in August that it had begun test-loading the
  missiles on Super Hornets, and aims for "at least limited service no later than
  2028" (The War Zone).
- **What the Pentagon committed to, and what it is seeking.** The Department's release
  of 13 May 2026: "once Castelion achieves testing and validation, the Department will
  award a two-year multi-year procurement contract for a minimum of 500 Blackbeard
  missiles annually, with options to extend for up to five years", and "the Department
  is actively seeking the necessary authorizations and appropriations to purchase over
  12,000 Blackbeard missiles over five years". The War Zone shortens this to "a
  framework agreement to buy 12,000". An earlier build of the film followed that
  wording and called the 12,000 the framework. It is not: it is an ask. The film now
  says "seeking", shows the 500-a-year minimum in the rail and on the plate, and keeps
  the 12,000 dots as the number sought.
- **No price per round.** An earlier build divided $43.65 million by 444 and said
  "roughly $98,000 a round". That figure is withdrawn. The $43.65 million is the first
  award on an order worth up to $200 million, and the Navy's 2027 budget request asks
  $156 million for 353 missiles of the same program (Military Times, Naval News),
  which is about $440,000 each. No unit price has been published, and the film gives
  none.
- **Arithmetic.** 12,000 over five years is shown as an even 2,400 a year because no
  schedule has been published. 14 to 25 September is eleven days.
- **Not released.** Speed (hypersonic means above Mach 5), how many a jet will carry
  and on which stations, and whether one has been launched from the aircraft. Castelion
  has hinted at a range of just under 500 miles and the Navy program at 200 nautical
  miles; neither is a measured figure, and the film gives no range.
- **Drawn, not reported.** The aircraft, the rounds and where they hang: the footage
  shows missiles under the wings, and the film draws one under each.
